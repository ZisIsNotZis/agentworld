# Validation strategy

## Validation claim

The project validates explicit mechanisms and budget-allocation methods. It does not validate a run because the resulting story sounds plausible.

## Quantitative state contract

Every comparison-relevant semantic property must project into numerical features. Each feature definition includes:

- name and stable identifier;
- source fields or evidence;
- transformation and aggregation;
- unit, bounds, missing-value behavior, and uncertainty;
- version and calibration dataset;
- failure modes and information loss.

Raw text may support an interpretation, but a comparison must cite the metric version and input evidence.

## Metric families

Initial metric families should cover:

- population, mortality, morbidity, and age structure;
- energy, food, material stocks, and production;
- wealth, coercive power, and their distributions;
- network connectivity, dependence, trust, and conflict;
- knowledge, skill, technology, and diffusion;
- institutions, compliance, enforcement cost, and stability;
- actor goal progress, strategy failure, and behavioral diversity;
- uncertainty, causal reach, and computation consumed.

Metrics form a vector. Scalar aggregation is allowed only when weights and sensitivity are visible. Branch selection also preserves diversity so one optimized score does not collapse the simulated world into a narrow artifact.

## Declared comparison metric: viability-referenced viability order (`vrd/2`)

M2b (ticket 37, 2026-10-18) rejects the R-anchored fidelity objective for VS2; M2c part 1 (ticket 38,
2026-10-18) repairs the objective's declared defect and declares `vrd/2`, the current objective. The
frozen eight-indicator divergence vector (`internal/experiment/fidelity.go:54`) remains a diagnostic;
it is no longer the target a run is asked to approach. Evidence: [`.scratch/38-m2c-preregistration/spec.md`](../.scratch/38-m2c-preregistration/spec.md)
(repair, ordering tests, recorded verification) and [`.scratch/37-fidelity-metric/spec.md`](../.scratch/37-fidelity-metric/spec.md)
(the anchor and the withdrawn measure).

- **Identifier:** `vrd/2`.
- **Source fields:** a run's hourly projection `alive_by_hour`, `births_cum`, `deaths_starve_cum` over the declared horizon `[0,H]` (samples `0..H`).
- **State vector:** `H_x = Σ alive_x(t)` (actor-hours), `B_x = births_x(H)`, `S_x = starvation deaths_x(H)`, `N0 = alive_x(0)`, and the persistence predicate `e_x = 1` iff `alive_x(H) = 0` (extinct at the terminal sample), else `0`.
- **Anchor:** `T` is the mean `(H,B,S,N0)` of the recorded no-AI control (arm `A0`) at the same fixture, seed and horizon; the viability set is `{H ≥ H_T, B ≥ B_T, S ≤ S_T}`. The anchor is worth approaching because it is the outcome the world already reaches with no cognitive intervention — population survives to the horizon, reproduces at its own rate and suffers no starvation deaths — not because any reference run does it. It is a floor, not an optimum, and it is deterministic, so it costs no live spend to re-measure.
- **Transformation and order:** the four components `d_H = max(0,(H_T−H_x)/H_T)`, `d_S = max(0,(S_x−S_T)/N0)`, `d_B = max(0,(B_T−B_x)/B_T)` and `e_x` form the key `(e, d_H, d_S, d_B)`, compared **lexicographically** in that order, which is the declared priority: population survived, then deaths avoided, then stability (proxied by reproduction at the control's rate). Lower is more viable in every tier; a run is at or above the floor iff its key is `(0,0,0,0)`.
- **Unit and bounds:** each component is dimensionless in `[0,1]`; the measure is a four-tuple, not a scalar, so no total is reported and no weight exists to declare or tune. The components are fractions of the control's actor-hours and births and starvation deaths per founding actor.
- **Missing-value behavior:** `vrd/2` is undefined — refused, never defaulted to zero — unless `H_T > 0`, `B_T > 0`, `alive_T(H) > 0` and `N0 > 0`. A fixture whose control is not viable must declare a substitute floor in its own ticket and may never substitute R.
- **Uncertainty:** per-replicate keys; the control is deterministic at the recorded fixtures, so the floor carries no sampling noise. A tier difference is a separation only when it clears the wider single-condition replicate envelope half-width of the two arms (ticket 35's band statistic applied to a horizon-scoped component), tier 1 requires unanimity across replicates, and ties are reported as ties; the full decision rule is pre-declared in the M2c criterion (ticket 38, section 7).
- **Version and calibration:** `vrd/2`, declared 2026-10-18 by ticket 38 as a repair of `vrd/1`: no component definition, weight or anchor changed, and the aggregation alone changed from an equal-weight sum to the declared precedence order. There is no external calibration dataset — the anchor is the run's own control, so the measure is model-conditional under P14. The sensitivity check is the six permutations of tiers 2–4 plus the terminal-sample convention; neither is a search for a setting that passes.
- **Failure modes and information loss:** the identity, kin and diversity of survivors, age-death timing, wealth and granary distributions, exchange, action mix and decision-error rate are not represented. An arm that exceeds the floor on every component sits at the floor and is indistinguishable from the control, so `vrd/2` cannot prefer growth over persistence; a growth objective needs a declared additional term. The order cannot express a tradeoff between tiers: one actor-hour of survival outranks any reproduction difference, by construction. The stability tier is a reproduction proxy, not a churn or variance measure, and the persistence predicate is read at the terminal sample, so a run that is alive at `h=143` and extinct at `h=H` ranks below every persisting run.
- **Withdrawn — `vrd/1`** (ticket 37, 2026-10-18): the equal-weight sum `D = 1·max(0,(H_T−H_x)/H_T) + 1·max(0,(B_T−B_x)/max(1,B_T)) + 1·max(0,(S_x−S_T)/N0)`. It is withdrawn because its birth term outranked its survival term: with `B_T = 2`, one birth was worth half the survival axis, so a sterile but fully surviving world scored 1.000 while an extinct world with one birth scored 0.5963, and a surviving world with one birth and one starvation death scored 0.6000 — worse than the extinct one. The defect is structural, not a weight choice: an additive form would need `w_H/w_B > 1/d_H(extinction)` (10.39 at the recorded `R` replicate 3, and unbounded as extinction moves later), and a declared floored-fertility variant at 10:1 weights was measured to fail the same test. Its recorded values are `vrd/1` numbers and are not comparable with `vrd/2` keys; the recorded arm *order* they produced is invariant under the repair at the one recorded matrix (0 of 15 arm pairs changed), which is why ticket 37's verdicts stand (evidence: `.scratch/38-m2c-preregistration/spec.md` sections R2–R3, R5).

The diagnostic divergence vector's reference replicate is undeclared: `referenceRun` matches on arm, fixture and seed only (`cmd/vs2/report.go:455`) and `sortRuns` does not order by replicate, so a report re-run can select a different R replicate. Ticket 37 found the recorded ticket-35 rows were computed against R replicate 3; the defect is recorded there and is not repaired in M2b, because repairing it would change recorded diagnostic rows and the EC-3/EC-4 inputs.

## Invariants

Automated checks should cover:

- conservation of modeled resources;
- valid births, deaths, identities, and relationships;
- ownership, debt, and transaction balance;
- authorized causes for state changes;
- legal information provenance for beliefs and actions;
- deterministic replay for covered operations;
- no mutation from rejected intentions;
- valid strategy and rule versions at every event;
- checkpoint and event-log consistency.

## Cognitive-allocation experiments

Given the same initial checkpoint, random streams, compute budget, and model access, compare allocation policies such as:

- fixed periodic waking;
- random sampling;
- structural-importance heuristics;
- anomaly-triggered waking;
- expected-value scheduling;
- mixtures with protected exploration.

Measure:

- model calls, tokens, CPU time, memory, and wall time;
- detected strategy failures;
- information-boundary violations;
- actor plausibility audits;
- macro-state divergence from a higher-budget reference;
- rare but consequential events captured or missed;
- realized value and calibration error of predicted priority.

## Beam-style development runs

At selected checkpoints, run several engine or allocation strategies in parallel. Periodically evaluate their metric vectors, causal validity, and diversity.

A beam controller may prune branches only under a declared rule. Preserve branch metadata and representative rejected branches so selection bias remains inspectable.

Use a higher-budget reference only as a comparative approximation, never as unquestioned ground truth.

## Human and social plausibility

Evaluate whether behavior follows available information, motives, ability, relationships, incentives, and institutions. Test cooperation and exploitation under scarcity, anonymity, unequal power, enforcement, public-goods dilemmas, rumor, and external threat.

A peaceful or unfamiliar society is not automatically invalid. It is valid only if the mechanisms and maintenance costs that sustain it are inspectable.

## Historical calibration

Compare distributions and mechanisms rather than exact named events. Candidate checks include carrying capacity, mortality, settlement growth, disease transmission, trade, inequality, state capacity, conflict, knowledge accumulation, and technology adoption.

Hold out periods or regions where practical. Prevent actor policies from receiving future historical outcomes.

## Model diagnostics

Use:

- ablation of supposedly important mechanisms;
- parameter sensitivity and uncertainty analysis;
- independent implementations of critical rules;
- adversarial seeds and red-team societies;
- random audits of low-priority actors;
- macro-to-micro reconciliation;
- causal backtracking from late anomalies to earliest divergence.

## Acceptance discipline

Every reported result states:

- code, rule, strategy, metric, and seed versions;
- branch ancestry and checkpoint compatibility;
- budget and model configuration;
- observed result distribution;
- sensitivity and known limitations;
- whether the claim is internal consistency, historical calibration, or model-conditional policy evidence.

## Mind-in-the-loop VS1 (M1) evidence

Revision `vs1-hermetic-fake/2` (base policy `natural/7`, world mechanism v6, no authorised spend); evidence artifact `.tmp/21-vs1-evaluation.txt`. Every result is `synthetic/model-conditional`.

### What VS1 implements

VS1 is the first slice in which a model is part of the world's causal chain. Two AI roles exist **beside** the world, never inside it; the seam is nil-tolerant and, with it absent, every recorded world, manifest, and policy byte is unchanged.

- **Role A — an actor's mind at the declared claim-instant seam.** At the declared `natural.claim` instant (`h+1µs`) one selected actor's action (`gather | eat-stored | provision | wait`) is chosen by a model call whose input is exactly that actor's frozen observation, encoded through an allow-list encoder that cannot reach `internal/world`. The existing kernel and contracts validate and commit the action; every refusal path mutates nothing.
- **Role B — in-run modification of behaviour logic as versioned strategy data.** From declared trajectory-evidence aggregates the model proposes a **strategy patch**: a typed integer delta vector over the bounded parameter contract, never code. The patch is validated, run through a deterministic sandbox counterfactual (patched versus parent, seam disabled), applied atomically to the roster at a quiescent boundary when admissible, measured, and rolled back when the declared criterion fails.
- **The recorded-call ledger.** Every call is an append-only, hash-chained record of the canonical request bytes and hash, the full recorded response, model id, endpoint, sampling parameters, usage, cost, latency, attempt index, and status; it is flushed before the response is consumed and bound by the checkpoint manifest, so a used call cannot be unrecorded.
- **Replay that resolves from the ledger and never calls a model.** Replay re-presents each request hash and resolves the recorded response; a miss is a hard error and replay mode is incapable of network access.
- **Cost accounting with four instrumented fail-loud ceiling rows.** Calls, tokens, cost, and latency are reported per call, per actor-hour, and per run under four typed ceilings that halt the run rather than silently substituting a cheap decision.
- **The `propose → validate → sandbox → apply → measure → mandatory rollback` lifecycle** with lineage, proposer, parent ref, validation verdict, sandbox verdict, apply boundary, affected actors, and rollback recorded and surviving restart.
- **The observation boundary.** Every Role A request is built from the actor's own observation; no privileged state, no other actor's private state, and no world reader. Role B packets carry only the declared aggregate fields.

1. **Newborn inheritance after apply.** The apply binds the living roster at the audit boundary; a newborn's first claim before the next rebind takes the frozen fallback. Inheritance at birth is not implemented.
2. **Single-`ApplyHour` replay.** A bundle restores the generation that took effect at its recorded boundary; an apply following a mid-run rollback within the same run is not yet replayed from a single bundle.
3. **The live audit set.** The live Role B loop audits `{24,48,72,96,120,144}`; `h168` is the terminal horizon (a 24-hour sandbox fork would exceed the frozen 168-hour envelope), so it is the measurement boundary rather than a live audit.
4. **Extinct-roster audits are no-ops.** An audit hour with no living roster records a zero-cost `no-effect` audit (`roster-extinct`) instead of a rebind.
5. **A3 funding.** All 12 declared A3 reference cells were funded in the hermetic matrix; the §11.3 A3 reduction (f=12 × seeds 0–3) applies only to an authorised-spend live run.
6. **The hermetic model.** Every arm runs the deterministic FakeClient; costs are modelled price-table charges over fake usage, never real spend.
7. **The fixtures are outside the declared replay scope.** The two scripted-proposer fixtures A2f/A2g are not replayed: the replay subset is the declared matrix's empty-generation AI cells, and both fixtures are hand-authored lifecycle harnesses, not matrix cells. Neither has an apply following a mid-run rollback, so EC-3 attributes them to this scope boundary, not to reduction 2.
8. **EC-5(a)'s `and replays byte-exact` clause is deferred.** With that boundary, the byte-exact genesis replay of a scripted fixture (and of a matrix cell carrying applied generation state) is left to the full-ledger replay lane.

Spec §14.1 also declares three further reductions — the sandbox gating regression is implemented as a deterministic ceiling-halt-or-extinction signal rather than a per-band comparison; EC-6's adversarial proof is a marker/key-set scan rather than a constructed privileged world; and a Role A intervention records the level-1 outcome, not the world verdict — and two further facts: the `R2` commit message's numeric detail is not independently checkable from the regenerated ledger, and the earlier reviews' numbered P2 lists are not stored as a single artifact.

### P15 findings

The §11.4 scale/ceiling probe (`P=32`, 256 founders, A1 arm, lowered `MaxCalls`) fired the typed fail-loud **cognitive-calls** ceiling with the ledger intact and replayable: threshold 32, observed 33 at h0, 64 ledger records, ledger chain verified. Verdict verbatim: the declared ceiling is a compute/authorised-spend ceiling and **the harness is outgrown, not a world design defect** (P15). No world mechanism depends on the budget.

The full quality gate at revision `c1d7810` also measured the `Makefile` test-harness timeouts against real compute on the 12-logical-CPU gate machine (go1.24.13, log `.tmp/vs1-gate-final.log`). The `race` target's arbitrary `-timeout 30m` pin was **inadequate**: the per-package race set measures 3552.14s (~59.20 min) and `internal/world` alone 2671.00s (~44.5 min), so 30m is 0.51x the full set and 0.67x its slowest package — a cap below measured compute that would turn the green suite into a false "test timed out" before it could finish. The pin is now a measured bound, `go test -race -timeout 90m ./...` (5400s = 1.52x the measured full-set wall, 2.02x the slowest package). The non-race `test` target measures 992.11s (full suite) and 985.44s (`internal/world`), so its 30m bound is 1.81x/1.83x measured compute — already at the P15 ≥1.5x standard — and is documented and left unchanged rather than inflated with unmeasured headroom. Neither finding changes a mechanism; the suite passes.
