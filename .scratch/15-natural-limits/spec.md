# Natural production limits and resource-determined population (design, v5)

Status: claimed

## Issue

Ticket 14 (v4 reproduction/genetics) delivered both frozen verdicts as SUPPORTED, but its outcome was structurally shaped by arbitrary design caps rather than by modeled nature: a global `MaxBirths = 16` ceiling, a legible newborn ID range (10001..10016) that silently caps the population at 32, and a runner allocation bound that exists only because of that range. Every enabled cell grew to 18–21 then collapsed to zero *because* the cap plus founder aging ended generational turnover — so the population trajectory was determined by our bookkeeping, not by resources.

Worse, the v3/v4 productive mechanism has no natural input at all: a capital point yields one food unit per hour forever, from nothing, so aggregate food is bounded only by per-actor capital bounds (KMax = 8) and the horizon. Carrying capacity is therefore not modeled; it is imposed by fixture bounds. Per P15 this is prohibited as a modeling device.

## Operator direction (recorded 2026-09-29)

If we hit the compute limit, then either the run succeeded (we outgrew the harness) or our design has a problem, and that must be analyzed case by case." ## Objective

Design (not implement) v5 such that population, production, and wealth are bounded **only** by modeled nature and by our compute, and such that hitting a compute ceiling is a diagnosable, reportable event rather than a fixture bound in disguise:

- remove the global birth cap and the ID-range cap; entity identity comes from the kernel allocator with no population ceiling other than compute;
- give production a real natural input so that carrying capacity is a consequence of the model (e.g. capital occupying land/slots, material or upkeep cost per unit of yield, patch regeneration taking time, or a combination) — with an explicit, integer-exact accounting story for every unit of food created;
- make reproduction resource-determined endogenously: the existing surplus gate, consent, kin rules, and costs already gate births, so with the caps gone the population trajectory must be decided by ecology (and decline/extinction must remain reachable);
- instrument the compute limits: the reporter must identify the binding ceiling, the value reached, and the hour, and the ticket must state the P15 case-by-case analysis rule for ceiling hits;
- specify what "we outgrew the harness" looks like versus "our design has a problem", with concrete diagnostics that distinguish them.

## Design questions (must be answered with exact numbers)

1. What natural input bounds production? Land/slots occupied by capital? Material units consumed? Upkeep food per point per hour? Patch regeneration and depletion? Each option's conservation identity must be exact.
2. What bounds per-actor capital and storage, and what is their natural referent (space, structure, spoilage)? If no referent exists, what replaces them?
3. How does the reproduction mechanism become cap-free while staying deterministic and replayable: unbounded entity IDs, unbounded roster, unbounded binding table, per-hour birth throughput limits (if any) with natural referents?
4. What is the expected qualitative behavior: exponential growth into the compute ceiling, logistic saturation at carrying capacity, oscillation, or collapse? State the honest expectation and the falsification criteria for each.
5. Compute instrumentation: which counters (actors, rows, events, history bytes, journal bytes, checkpoint bytes, fibers, wakes, wall/CPU per simulated hour) and which thresholds constitute "ceiling hit"; what diagnostic output distinguishes a successful overgrowth from a defect.
6. Does wealth need to become transferable now (inheritance, trade) or stay deferred to the trade/currency ticket? State which, and why, given that v5 changes what "wealth" bounds.
7. What observation/privacy discipline applies to the new production inputs (who can see land, stock, upkeep)?

## Frozen design: v5 "natural limits"

**Q1 — the natural input is LAND.** Slots are the production input: each of P patches has 8 slots, each slot is either **wild** (open-access standing stock, cap 1, v1–v4 semantics) or **cultivated** (owned by exactly one actor's capital point, granting into that owner's granary). The single natural production input is the **patch regeneration budget R per patch-hour** (soil fertility; R ∈ {0,8} in the matrix, R=8 = inherited q=8 semantics). Frozen pulse order in hour h replaces v4's produce/yield stages: **1 basal (verbatim v4 divisor loop) → 2 regen-split (rule 501)**: budget R is distributed — cultivated slots of *living* owners first (1 unit each into the granary, ascending slot order), then vacant wild slots fill ascending (exactly today's ProduceWild), remainder typed `regen-unrealized` → **3 spoilage (rule 502)** → **4 wear (rule 503, verbatim divisor loop)**, and when a capital point decays the owner's **lowest-index** cultivated slot reverts to wild (stock 0). Phases 0–7 (claim, gathered, bag decision, build, paired meal, birth request/reply) keep their frozen microsecond offsets. **Build (505)** now additionally claims, at completion with fresh-snapshot revalidation, the **lowest-index free wild slot with stock 0** in the builder's home patch; no free slot ⇒ typed refusal, zero mutation. Dead owners' slots go fallow (grant 0, budget term unrealized) until wear reverts them.

**Conservation identities (every created food unit accounted).** Per patch-hour: `R = cultivatedGranted + wildFilled + regenUnrealized`; lifetime: `R·pulses = gathered + wildStock(final) + cultivatedGranted + regenUnrealized`. Per granary lifetime: `cultivatedReceived = stock + storedMeals + spoiled + birthGranaryPaid` (the v4 `yield-unrealized` field is retired: with the harcap gone, grants are never lost to fullness). Capital: `invested = 2·created + wip`, `created − decayed = k`, and the new land identity `Σk(alive owners of a patch) = cultivatedSlots(patch) ≤ 8`. Energy verbatim v4 G3: `176 + 8·births + Σconsumed + ΣstoredMeals = Σenergy + ΣbasalSpent + ΣcapLoss`, per birth `4 granary + 4 booked energy → newborn energy 8`. Consequence: **carrying capacity is derived** — total inflow ≤ P·R/hour, each adult metabolizes 6/m ≈ 1/hour, so N* ≤ P·R ≈ 16 at P=2,R=8 minus spoilage, replacement builds and birth costs; the birth gate (k≥2) makes **land the direct bottleneck of reproduction**. Rejected for Q1: upkeep-multiplier (needs an unexplained 2× fertility premium), keeping v4 yield-from-nothing (fake carrying capacity, P15 violation in substance), central land adjudication (preinstalled institution, P10), random slot assignment (breaks determinism).

**Q2 — capital and storage bounds.** Capital k ∈ [0,8] with natural referent = land (an actor cannot hold more plots than its patch has slots; the patch total `Σk ≤ 8` is structural). The granary hard cap 8 is **replaced by spoilage**: each pulse `spoilDebt += stock; while spoilDebt ≥ S: stock−1, spoiled+1, spoilDebt−=S` with **S=24** (preserved food keeps ~one day; a declared nature convention like wear period 6, not a cap — sustainable stock is *derived*: inflow×S). Integer-exact declared bounds are horizon envelopes that cannot bind behavior before the horizon: `stock ∈ [0,1344]`, `spoilDebt ∈ [0,1367]`, `spoiled ∈ [0,1344]`; widened ledgers for cap-free births: `BasalSpentMax = 1008`, `BirthGranaryPaidMax = 672`. Bag 0..1, energy [0,12], hunger [0,24] kept verbatim. Honest disclosure: S=24 and the FIFO decay order are conventions inside a natural process (perishability; which plot is abandoned) — declared, frozen, and no behavior depends on hiding a cap behind them.

**Q3 — cap-free but deterministic and replayable.** Entity identity comes from the kernel's `sim.EntityAllocator` (monotonic uint64, typed failure only at MaxUint64), initialized at genesis to the highest fixed entity ID (2000+8P); newborn IDs are 17, 18, … with **no range and no cap**; the allocator's last ID is runner state cross-checked against the max roster ID at restore (mismatch fails closed). Roster/binding tables stay maps (v4 machinery already variable-length); every count assertion is removed (`MaxBirths`, the gather-claims cap, fixed fiber-count pulse checks become *set equality* of living actors ↔ fibers). Policy binding stays one frozen ref `natural/5` per actor. Genome draw position stays `7×(ordinal−1)` on stream `0x47454E33` with unbounded ordinal. **Throughput limits are time-referenced only** (one gather, one courtship = one request + at most one reply per actor-hour); everything else that gates births is ecology: consent thresholds, land availability, kin distance ≥ 3, same patch, the 8-unit conversion cost. Concurrent-birth ID collision keeps v4's typed lost-birth evidence (loser stages nothing, retries next hour, deterministic). All roster iterations sorted by ID so unbounded rosters stay exact; the O(n²) per-wake world read is *measured*, not capped (Q5).

**Q4 — honest expected behaviour.** Default fertility (R=8, births-on, 16 founders): **logistic saturation, possibly damped oscillation**, growth from 16 toward N* ≈ 10–16, births ≈ deaths turnover at the plateau, the founder die-off wave (h96–143) absorbed if land and surplus permit. Falsifiers, each reported verbatim: (a) sustained doubling over any 48 h window after h96 (would indicate an accounting bug — impossible under the inflow identity); (b) extinction before h168; (c) plateau with `regenUnrealized > 25%` of R *and* starvation deaths (production blocked = defect); (d) oscillation with growing amplitude at h168. R=0: extinction ≈ h11, zero births (the granary≥3 gate is unreachable with zero inflow). Births-off R=8: aging decline to 0 by ≤ h143 (v4-disabled shape). The 256-founder cell is expected to hit the compute ceiling while near its (larger) carrying capacity — food adequate, evidence accumulation is what exhausts. No band claims self-sustenance; extinction, collapse, saturation and ceiling are all first-class.

**Q5 — compute-ceiling instrumentation contract.** Ceilings are the existing constants, **values unchanged**: kernel `MaxHistoryEvents=65536`, `MaxHistoryPatches=262144`, `MaxHistoryValueNodes=524288`, `MaxHistoryBytes=134217728`, `MaxHistoryProjectionBytes=33554432`, `MaxHistoryProjectedMetrics=65536`; component `MaxSnapshotBytes=33554432`; sim `maxEventBytes=1048576`, `MaxValueBytes=1048576`; scheduler `MaxPortableBytes=8388608`, `MaxPortableFibers=65536`, `MaxPortableWakes=65536`; runner step ceiling `6048` (=4·168·9) and allocator overflow. The runner keeps an **hourly headroom ledger** (per ceiling: threshold, current value, measured rate, projected binding hour) and emits a typed `CeilingHit{ceiling, threshold, observed, hour, worldVersion, alive, births, deaths, last24hInflow/Consumption, regenUnrealized}` — fail-loud at the first export/checkpoint/commit that would exceed, or proactively when projected headroom < 1 hour; silent truncation anywhere is a stop condition. **Verdict table (printed, P15 case-by-case):** conservation green ∧ population ≥ ~0.9·N* (or sustained growth) ∧ turnover births≈deaths ∧ replay byte-identical to the hit hour ⇒ **"outgrew the harness"** (success; record and scale the harness — raise the kernel ceiling as infrastructure, never the mechanism); conservation green ∧ starvation deaths > births ∧ large `regenUnrealized` ⇒ **design defect** (production/land mis-specified); any conservation/replay/ownership/linkage failure ⇒ **implementation defect**; flat population with fiber/wake explosion ⇒ **runtime defect** (wake storm).

**Q6 — wealth transfer stays DEFERRED**, for three recorded reasons: the operator's sequence puts exchange/trade next and inheritance is institutional machinery (v4 already deferred it); v5's spoilage dissolves dead granaries (a dead actor's inaccessible stock rots to zero within 24 h per unit), removing the main pressure that made inheritance urgent; and inter-vivos transfer already exists as the ecological channel (each birth moves 4 granary units per parent into the newborn). Forward-compat note: granary stock stays a plain bounded integer so the trade ticket can later operate on it without a schema break.
